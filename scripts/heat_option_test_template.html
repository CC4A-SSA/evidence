Heat option test

Draft. Pending expert review. Test of draft decision 0005: how the base temperature of a crop heat slope changes yield loss. Built by scripts/heat_option_test.py.

The options

  • Now: every crop uses the maize slope (k 0.01 per degree day, Lobell et al. 2011, fitted above 30 °C) on degree days above the crop's moderate heat threshold heat_moderate_c.
  • A: refit the paper's k to heat_moderate_c. kA = kpaper × mean(degree days above paper base) ÷ mean(degree days above heat_moderate_c), over reference seasons (here the Kenya seasons).
  • B: keep the paper's own equation and base temperature (form and metric_base_c); tiers unchanged.
  • C: move heat_moderate_c (and the tier ladder with it) to the paper's base. Loss per season is the same as B; which seasons count as moderate, severe or extreme changes.

1. Loss against heat exposure

Each point is a typical heat window: daily maximum temperatures spread normally around the window mean, a fixed daily range, and hourly temperatures on a sine curve between Tmin and Tmax. The x axis is degree days above the crop's heat_moderate_c (rice 26 °C, maize 30 °C; wheat has none, so 30 °C is assumed).

Rice

Rice window 60 days. Option C gives the same line as B (Dong). Lyman: day degree days above 33 °C plus night degree days above 22 °C, here daily (Tmax − 33, Tmin − 22), not the paper's exact counting. Jian: % per day of exposure, a day counted when Tmax is 30–35 °C or above 35 °C; field-warming experiments, so a proxy. kA from .

Maize

Maize window 60 days. The tier (30 °C) and Lobell's base (30 °C) match, so now, A, B and C are the same line.

Wheat

Wheat window 40 days (assumed; wheat has no heat window or tier in cc4a-hazards). Tack et al. 2017 give % per +1 °C of season mean in Kansas dryland trials, not a degree-day slope, so a starting temperature must be assumed (slider). A cannot be computed without a degree-day slope; C has no tier to move.
